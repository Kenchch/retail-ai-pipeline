# Adoption measurement, workshop, and the monthly update

Owner: business engagement. The metrics below are computed by
`retail_pipeline/adoption.py` and published to `reports/adoption_report.md` by
every pipeline run — the definitions here and the definitions in the code are
the same definitions.

---

## Part 1 — Measurement

The steering group asks "is anyone using it" at the first review. "I think so"
is not an answer, and neither is a number nobody can define.

| Metric | Definition | Answers |
|---|---|---|
| **Reach** | Distinct users with ≥1 event in the last 4 weeks ÷ licensed users | Did it get into people's hands? |
| **Activation** | Users who have ever acted on a recommendation ÷ licensed users | Did it change what anyone did? |
| **Action rate** | `apply` events ÷ `view` events | Was it useful *per look*? |
| **CSAT** | Mean of 1–5 in-report feedback scores | How does it feel to use? |

**Activation is the one that matters.** A view changes nothing, and reach is easy
to move with an email.

**Action rate is a ratio of events, not of people**, so it stays comparable as
the user base grows. Reach going up usually pushes action rate *down* — new
users browse before they act — and a measure that punishes you for onboarding
people will get gamed.

**A metric with no data shows "–", never 0.** Nobody answering the feedback
survey is not the same as everybody being unhappy, and no views is not a 0%
action rate — there was nothing to act on.

Every metric is also cut by team, because the total hides the thing worth acting
on. This quarter the total is on target and one team sits at 50%.

### Where the events come from

| Event | Production source |
|---|---|
| `view` | Power BI / Fabric report usage metrics |
| `apply` | Merchandising tool audit log — a recommendation accepted onto a planogram or bundle |
| `feedback` | In-report feedback button → SharePoint list |

> The event log in this repository is generated by `scripts/get_data.py`, because
> the solution has not been deployed to real users. The schema is the production
> schema and the metrics are computed for real from it; swapping in a real
> extract is one line in `config.yaml`. This is stated here, in that script and
> in the README, because an adoption number of unclear provenance is worse than
> no adoption number.

### Cadence

Weekly to the delivery team, fortnightly to the Business Engagement Lead,
monthly to the steering group (headline four, trend, one decision), monthly to
each team lead — their own cut, not the average.

## Part 2 — The AI-literacy workshop

60 minutes, 8–15 people, mixed technical confidence, no prerequisites. Deck:
`workshop/ai_literacy_workshop.pptx`. Run twice so far, weeks 3 and 8.

**Objective — three things, not eight.** By the end everyone can (1) read a
recommendation and say whether to trust it, (2) name two things it cannot do,
and (3) knows how to give feedback. Explaining how the model works internally is
*deliberately not* an objective; nobody in the room has to build one.

| Time | Segment |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | **Open with a failure** — a real bad recommendation from the live tool. "Would you have shipped this?" |
| 0:10 | Why it happened — basket count, promotions, the text-similarity rows |
| 0:20 | Reading a row: baskets, confidence, lift — one number at a time |
| 0:30 | **Hands-on:** five real rows in pairs — trust / don't trust / need more info |
| 0:45 | Debrief. The rows that split the room are the whole workshop |
| 0:55 | What it can't do, where the feedback button is, one ask |

**Open with a failure, not a demo.** A polished demo puts the room in evaluation
mode — they spend the hour deciding whether to believe you. A real bad output
puts them in diagnosis mode and answers the question they are actually holding
("is this going to embarrass me?") in the first five minutes.

**Protect the hands-on 15 minutes.** If you are running late, cut the limits
segment and email it. Never cut the exercise.

**The exercise row that matters** is a seasonal best-seller with confidence 0.44
and lift 1.1. Every group has had someone trust it on confidence alone. That
moment teaches more than any slide.

**What happened:** weekly reach went 16% → 34% after the week-3 session and 27%
→ 44% after the week-8 session, and both held rather than decaying. A spike that
decays means people looked once, which is not adoption.

## Part 3 — Monthly update (June 2026)

*To Merchandising, Category Management, Online Trading, Marketing, Store Ops and
Data & AI. Kept to 300 words, because a 900-word update is not read.*

**Frequently Bought Together — month 3**

- **74% of you used it in the last four weeks**, against a 70% target.
- **77% have acted on at least one recommendation.** That is the number we
  actually care about; a view changes nothing.
- **Feedback sits at 4.2 out of 5** across 69 responses.
- **Action rate is 22.9% against a 25% target** — the one measure below target.
  The honest reason is that it fell as more people came on; new users browse
  before they act. If it hasn't recovered by August we will say so here rather
  than quietly retiring the target.

**What we learned.** The two workshops did more than any email — weekly reach
16% → 34% after the first and 27% → 44% after the second, and both held. A third
runs in July.

**Where it isn't working.** Store Ops is at 50% reach and 3.5 on feedback, the
only team below both. The comments are clear that the view is built around
catalogue decisions that team doesn't make. That is a fit problem and another
training session won't fix it. We meet the Store Ops lead in July to choose
between a store-level view and taking them out of scope. We would rather report
a smaller honest number than a larger diluted one.

**Next.** A/B test design for the incremental-revenue question, a July workshop,
and the category-hierarchy filter four of you have now asked for.

## Part 4 — For the steering group

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Delivered, in adoption |
| **Investment** | One engineer, three weeks, existing infrastructure, no new licences |
| **Coverage** | The full 3,803-line catalogue |
| **Evidence base** | 522,566 clean transaction lines; 3.6% of source rows rejected by automated quality rules |
| **Reach / Activation / CSAT** | 74.2% (target 70%) · 77.4% · 4.23 / 5 |

**The one thing to know:** adoption is ahead of target and the tool is trusted,
but we cannot yet put a revenue number against it. Attribution needs a
controlled test, and inventing a number we cannot defend would cost more
credibility than it buys.

**Decision requested:** approve a four-week A/B test in one category.

**Risk being managed:** one of five teams sits well below target because the
solution does not fit how they work — being resolved in July by adapting the
view or removing them from scope, not by more training.

### Roadmap

| When | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Jul 2026 | A/B test design — one category, four weeks, on vs off | The only defensible route to an incremental-revenue number |
| Jul 2026 | Store Ops: adapt the view or descope | A target nobody owns is not a target |
| Aug 2026 | Category hierarchy in the product dimension | Four teams have asked |
| Aug 2026 | Tune `min_support_count` against observed action rate | Currently judgement; this makes it evidence |
| Q4 2026 | Customer-level personalisation — a decision point, not a commitment | Only worth revisiting if the identifier coverage problem is solved |

**Not on the roadmap:** automated pricing or promotion actions. Someone asks
every review. The answer stays no until we can attribute revenue to a
recommendation, and that starts with the A/B test.
